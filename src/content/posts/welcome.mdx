There is a gap between what we write in journal articles and what we actually
know. Papers are forced to be tidy: a clean question, a single estimator, a
table of robustness checks. The mess — the dead-end specifications, the data
quirks, the unresolved doubts — gets compressed out.

This site is where I plan to keep the mess.

## What you can expect

Three kinds of posts:

1. **Notes on methods.** Things I wish someone had explained to me when I was
   learning honest difference-in-differences, repeat-sales indices, or how to
   handle right-censoring in transaction data.
2. **Field reports.** What it actually looks like to assemble a panel from
   thirty county assessor websites.
3. **Argued opinions.** Occasionally, a piece that takes a position on
   something — the fifty-year mortgage, the housing-as-asset framing, the
   incentive structure of academic publishing. Argued, not just asserted.

## What you won't find

No newsletter. No schedule. No "thread" at the top of every post telling you
what is in the post you are about to read. If a post is worth your time, the
opening paragraph will earn it; if it is not, no amount of summary will
rescue it.

> The standard for publishing here is *would I send this to a smart friend
> and not be embarrassed?* That bar is harder than it sounds.

If you want to be told when something new goes up, the easiest path is to
[email me](/contact) and ask. I keep a small list and use it once or twice a
year.
